NOTES
Task 1: Lucy is the completed example. Ben: plays | is drawing; They: eat | —. The dash is printed, not answered or graded. Accept only the verb forms without objects; the source distinguishes Saturday routines from the Sunday scene.
Task 2: The first turn establishes the relevant time frame; alternatives are grammatically sound but not suitable answers to the questions. The situations in items 1–4 provide context, but do not independently prove a specific activity; the contrast is the time frame requested by the question.
Task 3: The starred choice in each pair is the correct answer. Item 5 uses the audible song and item 6 uses an immediate refusal as present-action cues. Item 3's 'never' and 'on Sundays' establish a routine, not a single present event.
Task 4: 0: plays; 1: am drawing; 2: doesn't swim (also accept does not swim); 3: Do | watch; 4: go; 5: is talking. The invitation/refusal in item 1 and the phone-use refusal in item 5 signal ongoing actions. For item 2, 'No' answers the habitual question.
Task 5: Model 1: 'Ben plays football in the park on Saturdays. He is drawing a picture for Grandma at Grandma's house.' Model 2: 'Lucy swims at the sports centre on Saturdays. She is reading a story in the garden.' Accept other grammatical two-sentence responses expressing the matching Saturday habit and Sunday 3 p.m. action from the table. Do not accept a response that repeats the friend's incorrect claim as the current action.
Task 6: Examples only, not fixed answers: 'I play tennis on Sundays. I read a story on Sundays. I am drawing a picture now. My sister is watching television now.' Grade two true Sunday-habit sentences in the simple present and two true current-action sentences in the present continuous, covering the pupil and one family member; accept other appropriate content and correct forms.
